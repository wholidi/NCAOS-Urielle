That opens up a different dimension:

- patterns of repeated containment or escalation
- stability vs deviation over time

From the audit perspective, those patterns could serve as indirect but meaningful evidence of control effectiveness, even without internal state visibility.

- defining a minimal but stable event schema
- ensuring consistency across event types and sequences
- introducing optional context fields (identity, asset, session) in a way that enhances interpretation without breaking the boundary

What I find particularly strong in this direction is that it keeps the separation clean:
- NCAOS defines and enforces the boundary
- Urielle audit layer interprets structured signals into governance evidence

And the more structured and consistent the signal layer becomes, the stronger the audit conclusions can be — without needing to expand inward.
