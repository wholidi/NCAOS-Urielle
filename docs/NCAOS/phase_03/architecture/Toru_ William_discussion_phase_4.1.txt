What we’ve validated through this PoC is quite significant: the boundary is sufficient for external interpretation, and the audit layer can generate meaningful governance artifacts without internal access. That confirms that the decoupled model works not only conceptually, but in practice.

I also see this as a natural transition point into the next phase, and I agree that the focus should now move toward the interface.

From my perspective, the priority would be:
- Moving toward a minimal but stable interface definition
- Expanding the event surface carefully to test consistency
- Observing how additional context (such as identity or asset references) affects audit depth — while keeping the boundary strictly intact

On the question of how far we can push from signal to audit evidence while preserving the boundary — I think there is quite a lot of room to explore.

The key is not increasing internal visibility, but increasing the structure and quality of the signals themselves.

In that sense:
- The depth of audit is largely a function of how well events are defined and structured
- Strong consistency across event sequences can serve as indirect but sufficient evidence
- Additional context can enhance interpretation without breaking the boundary

From this perspective, the boundary is not a limitation, but what actually makes the model robust and auditable.

So rather than expanding inward, I see the next phase as refining the signal layer until it can support stronger audit conclusions on its own.
